Waypoint GO · Grand Finale pitch · Storyboard v4: rebuilt on the team's own flow · 27 October 2026 · 10 minutes

The pitch in one line: people decide together, and on record

A delivery day breaks in hundreds of small ways. Every break lands on one person's phone. GO does not replace the people who fix them. It gives each of them the tools to decide together, quickly, and on record.

The arc

The chess move from our notes: show the problem, let the audience think of the obvious answers, show why each one fails, then show what we did. The logo reveal is the pivot from problem to answer. The close returns to the opening scene, so the "we need this" feeling lands on a story the audience already cares about.

The link between problem and answer: the ring

No red. The link comes from our own logo animation, where "Ground Operations" collapses into a broken, dashed ring that closes into the O of GO. We use that ring as the one motif of the deck.

Open

A problem nobody has answered yet. Night colour, dashed like the logo's ring.

Stays open

The obvious answers. Their rings fade but never close.

Closing

Hover to see it. Same ring, same position, morphs shut and turns GO green.

Closed

Answered. On the last slide every closed ring merges into the O of GO.

Rules for the deck

  • Green appears only when a ring closes. Before the pivot the deck is white and night only, so green itself starts to mean "answered".
  • A ring keeps its exact position from the problem slide to its answer slide. Name both shapes !!ring-demand, !!ring-load, !!ring-talk, !!ring-0652 so Morph pairs them.
  • Build the ring from the logo's own dash segments (arc shapes), not a dashed outline: Morph animates an arc's angle, but only cross-fades a change of dash style. Test both in PowerPoint and keep whichever looks smoother.
  • At most three open rings on screen at once. Too many open questions confuse more than they intrigue.
  • The speaker names the device once, on slide 3: "Hold on to these three." The cold-listener test showed a lone ring on slide 1 means nothing until then.
  • Never use the word "ring" in the script for anything else. An alert is "calls the dispatcher", not "rings".

Why it works

  • Closure. A broken circle is still read as a circle, so the eye wants to complete it. The audience feels the gap before we name it.
  • Open loops. An unanswered question holds attention until it is closed. The 06:52 case opens on slide 1 and closes on slide 10.
  • A motif that returns means the audience does not have to work to reconnect a problem with its answer. The ring does it for them.
  • It is our brand. The logo reveal teaches the motif, so the pivot slide and the final slide both mean something.

Hard questions, and our answers

The judge's five hardest questions, answered from the rules as built. Whoever takes Q&A should know these cold.

QuestionAnswer
At 06:52 on the Kandy corridor the van has no signal. What happens?The driver's app works fully offline: taps, messages and voice notes wait on the phone and send when the signal returns, and nothing is lost. GO keeps estimating arrival from the last known position, so a held-up van still shows late. The watch asks the store anyway. If the driver has not seen a message after 10 minutes, the dispatcher is told to phone (R-NOT-18).
Who guarantees the cold chain for store one's goods?Chilled goods never leave the refrigerated van for another store. Store two is only asked whether the crew may unload and reload at its dock to reach its own goods (R-ISS-12). Store one's order moves to the next operating day and goes first (R-EXE-31), and store one is told.
How much better than today's spreadsheet, and what is the false-alarm rate?The slide 12 numbers, each against its baseline, from the ML owner. Have the exact figures and the test period ready.
If we approve tomorrow, what do the first 30 days look like?The Kandy depot: weeks 1-4 beside today's process, weeks 5-8 live. Drivers use their own phones, install it from the browser, and get a guided tour on the first run; loaders use a shared tablet, dispatchers a desktop. Stop if the four measures on slide 14 do not move.
Does "how often each option was chosen" just repeat old habits? Who is accountable?Cards are never preselected and never act. Until a situation has 20 decided cases they come from rules, not history. Every decision is recorded with the person and their reason, and outcomes are measured (delivered next run, a store left waiting twice), not just which card was tapped.
What is the planner, and how close to the best is it?A constraint-checked allocation engine, then a cost stage (ALNS) that keeps the served set, measured against an exact solver (CP-SAT, SCIP) and OR-Tools on the peak day, historic and synthetic days (docs/issues/219-planning-v2/benchmark). Have the gap to the exact maximum ready.
What is Jev, where does it run, and how accurate is it?A small decision model that only picks one of the problem types code allows, and writes no text. It reads masked text: no names, phones or ids. 58 of 60 on our labelled set (English 41/41, Sinhala 6/6, Tamil 6/6, Singlish 5/7), median 0.5 s. The set is small and written by us, so production scores decide. Off unless a depot agrees.
How does data get in, and how hard is a second depot?Orders and products come from the warehouse API (polled and matched); reference data (outlets, vehicles, calendar) is imported as a versioned, all-or-nothing publish. A depot is reference data plus scope grants.
Do 120 store managers need devices and training? What if they do not answer?A phone or the counter desktop, in the browser. A question unanswered by its deadline expires once, and the dispatcher is told to phone (R-ISS-13); today's rules then apply.
Who supports it after the competition?Team answer needed: support model, hosting (one server, Docker Compose), and handover.

1:00Act 3 · closes !!ring-talkR3R9

On screen

Say

Third, communication. We made it text based: one conversation per trip, for everyone on it. (click) Why text? Freedom: people say what really happened, not only what a form allows. Low LLM consumption: a message needs no AI to arrive, so AI is used only where it helps. And audit: every word has a name and a time, for audit later and for live decisions now. (click) But typing is friction, especially for a driver. So we added three things: reports in one tap, templates of ready replies, and voice. (click) And while the van moves, the phone goes dark. When it stops, messages are read aloud in Sinhala, Tamil or English, and the driver answers with a voice note. (click) When someone writes in their own words, Jev sorts it into a known problem. And we keep two words apart everywhere: a report is what someone says from the field; an issue is the problem GO records from it, with an owner until it is closed.

Sources behind the structure

Arc: Duarte's "what is / what could be" structure (Duarte, GoReact) and the strategic narrative, which opens with the change in the world rather than the product (Echai, Nektar). Person decides: exceptions touching money, safety or a customer are flagged and drafted by the system and decided by a person (Numeo, Expedock; vendor sources). Ring: closure (Smashing Magazine), open loops (ModelThinkers; the evidence is mixed, so we use it as craft, not science), recurring motifs (Ethos3). Morph pairing with "!!" names: Microsoft.
