# Waypoint GO · Pitch script

For: a technical audience seeing GO for the first time. 10 minutes.
Numbers in brackets are the slides in **GO-pitch-flow.pptx**. **(click)** means advance one slide. **(pause)** means a full breath.

---

## Waypoint today [1-2]

Five o'clock in the morning at Peliyagoda.

A hundred and twenty stores are waiting for their goods. Fresh supermarkets, Style stores in malls, Tech stores with fragile appliances. Sixty vehicles, and only sixteen can carry chilled goods.

**(click)**

And behind all of it: one spreadsheet, a phone, and a dispatcher's memory. Once a van leaves the depot, nobody can see it. It works today, because people work very hard.

## Three problems [3-5]

Three problems stood out.

First: on peak days, before a festival, there are more orders than vehicles. Somebody has to decide which stores wait, and explain why.

**(click)**

Second: one dispatcher carries up to a hundred and twenty trips a day. Planning, comparing, deferring, watching, in one head, at three in the morning.

**(click)**

And third: when one van is late, the phone calls start. One store after another, while every store's delivery window keeps closing.

## Waypoint GO [6]

**(click, the logo plays)**

So we built Waypoint GO. Ground Operations: everything between the order and the shelf.

## Same hiker, better gear [7-8]

**(click)**

Waypoint today is a good hiker in a good pair of shoes. They reach the top on skill and effort.

**(click)**

GO gives the same hiker the gear. Binoculars, to see trouble early. A map of every trip. A compass that explains every plan. A radio that connects everyone. We don't replace Waypoint's people, and we don't automate their decisions away. **Their know-how. Our tools.**

## Four people, one app [9-11]

**(click)**

One app, for the four people who touch every order. The dispatcher, on a laptop in the planning office.

**(click)**

The loader, on a shared tablet at the dock.

**(click)**

And the store manager and the driver, on their phones. The driver's app keeps working with no signal in the hills.

Now, problem by problem.

## Problem 1 · More orders than vehicles [12-15]

**(click)**

Orders close at four in the afternoon, and the dispatcher asks GO for a plan. In seconds, every order is placed on a vehicle and a trip, within weight, volume, refrigeration, mall hours and fuel quota.

**(click)**

Some orders still won't fit. Every one of them is explained in plain words: the rule that stopped it, where it came closest, and the smallest change that would make room. No more "because the spreadsheet said so".

**(click)**

And because peaks are predictable, the forecast looks ten weeks ahead, so the admin can hire vehicles before the festival, not on the morning.

**(click)**

But the best peak is the one you spread out. A store manager orders for Wednesday the fourth. Other stores in that district already have deliveries on the third and the fifth, so GO suggests those days: a vehicle is going there anyway. Only a suggestion; the store manager decides. Fewer half-empty trips.

## Problem 2 · One dispatcher, up to 120 trips [16-19]

**(click)**

Once the vans leave, GO's watcher follows every trip live, against every store's window. It stays quiet until something needs a decision, and it has often already asked the store, so the answer is waiting.

**(click)**

Everything that needs the dispatcher lands on one focus page: **Needs you**. One list, one next step at a time. Asking is automatic. **Deciding is not.**

**(click)**

And when they plan, nothing is locked: compare two plans, drag a stop to another trip, defer an order.

**(click)**

GO checks every change against its own rules. GO brings the tools. The dispatcher brings what no dataset holds: which manager opens late, which road floods in the monsoon.

## Problem 3 · One late van, a chain of calls [20-31]

**(click)**

Problem three is where operations really break. A van arrives on time, but the store closed early today. A van breaks down with chilled goods inside. The loader says everything was loaded; the store says three cartons are missing. A driver delivers with no signal, while the dispatcher has already skipped that store. We catalogued **four hundred and eighty-five** cases like these.

**(click)**

We did not build a screen for each one. Every case goes through the same five tools. A report, in one tap, with photos. The watcher, which spots trouble before anyone calls. GO asking the stores by itself. One room for the trip. And Needs you, where a person decides. **Four hundred and eighty-five cases. Five tools. One record.**

**(click)**

What changes is the clock. Some problems can be settled later: missing goods, damage, a shortage. They become an issue with an owner, tracked to the end. Others can't wait: a window is closing right now. That needs a live decision, in minutes. Let's follow one.

**(click)**

A van has five stores. Two are delivered. Then traffic: store three's window will close before the van gets there.

**(click)**

Easy, just skip it? No. The van was loaded last in, first out, so store three's goods sit in front of store four's. To skip store three, the crew must unload its goods at store four, take store four's, and load store three's back in. One decision, and it needs the dispatcher, the driver and two store managers to agree. In the next ten minutes.

**(click)**

Today, that decision is made by phone. The dispatcher calls the driver, then store three, then store four, then the driver again. One call at a time. Nobody sees the whole picture, and by evening nothing is on record.

**(click)**

There are three obvious ways to fix that.

**(click)**

Let AI decide everything? When it leaves the same store waiting two days running, who answers for it? Buy a routing or tracking tool? It can move the van, but it can't get store four to lend its dock. Start a WhatsApp group? Fast, but nothing in it is structured, and nothing is on record.

**(click)**

So every trip gets one room. The dispatcher, the driver and every store manager on that trip, in one conversation, live. GO takes part: it asks the stores and suggests the next step. **But a person decides.**

**(click)**

Why a conversation, and not a form? Freedom: people say what really happened, not only what a form allows. Low AI cost: a message needs no AI to arrive, so AI is used only where it helps. And audit: every word has a name and a time.

**(click)**

But typing is friction, especially for a driver. So: reports in one tap. Ready replies that fit the moment. And voice: notes are transcribed, messages are read aloud.

**(click)**

Back to our trip. GO asks store three: can someone receive later? A voice note comes back: no, our staff leave at seven thirty. GO suggests the next step: ask store four for its dock. The dispatcher sends it. Store four taps yes. The driver sees the new plan on the phone. **Minutes, not twenty calls, and every word on record.**

## Made for the driver [32]

**(click)**

For the driver, the first rule is that GO works with no signal. The day's trip is on the phone before the van leaves; deliveries, photos and signatures are sent when the signal returns. While the van moves, the screen goes dark: nothing to read, nothing to tap. When it stops: the next stop, one action, and a Fuel QR that works offline. And anyone can leave the driver a voice note, so the driver listens instead of reading.

## The dispatcher's MCP [33]

**(click)**

Two more tools. The dispatcher can connect their own AI assistant to GO through MCP, and simply ask: which trips are at risk this morning? It reads the day within the permissions the dispatcher already has, and it never acts alone: even raising an issue waits for their confirm.

## The admin's console [34]

**(click)**

And the admin runs the business without a developer: users and roles, permission policies written like a cloud console, vehicles and planning parameters, changed without a new release. Every change, like every decision in GO, is audited: who, what, when, and why.

## Design [35]

**(click)**

Every screen was designed for people who start at three in the morning: dark screens, one action per screen, slide to confirm, three languages.

## Built [36]

**(click)**

And it's not a prototype. It's live, at waypointgo.live. Four installable role apps that work offline, a Spring Boot backend split into business modules, and PostgreSQL that enforces every permission on every row. Every one of those four hundred and eighty-five edge cases has its rule and its test.

## Intelligence [37]

**(click)**

Underneath are our own models. They flag seven in ten late deliveries before the van even leaves. They forecast demand ten weeks ahead, within about three per cent. And on the busiest day in the data, the planner serves [N] of eighty-five orders, and explains every one that waits. Rules first, then our models, and a person decides.

## Future works [38]

**(click)**

And this is where it goes next. Enterprises are moving to AI agents. The MCP the dispatcher uses today is the bridge: every GO tool can become a tool for Waypoint's agents, under the same permissions and the same audit trail. When Waypoint is ready for the agentic enterprise, GO is already plugged in.

**(click)**

Same people. Same know-how. Better tools.
